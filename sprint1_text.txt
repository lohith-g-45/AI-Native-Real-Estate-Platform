1. Purpose and Scope
This document takes the Sprint 1 row of the Master PRD's Initial Sprint Plan (Section 15.5) and turns it into
requirements the team can build against. Sprint 1 is the foundation sprint — it's where we stand up the live,
deployable shell of the product that every sprint after this one (2 through 10) builds on top of.
Under the Master PRD's Iterative Scope Rule (Section 15), this plan should be read as an initial forecast rather
than a locked waterfall schedule. Stories can be split or reshaped after the sprint demo based on what we learn.
The one hard rule: if a P0 requirement needs to change, that change has to be reflected in a newly approved
PRD version before we drop anything.

2. Governance Carried Over from the Master PRD
Every story pulled into Sprint 1 has to follow the same rules laid out in Master PRD Sections 15.2 through 15.4.
Nothing changes here — this is just a reminder of what "ready" and "done" mean going into this sprint.
2.1 Definition of Ready
●
The user, the outcome, and the business reason behind the story are all clear.
●
Acceptance criteria and the mandatory demo path are written down.
●
Data, legal/policy, and dependency assumptions have been identified.
●
AI autonomy level, confidence policy, fallback behaviour, and audit requirements are defined.
●
Mobile-browser behaviour and API impact are understood before work starts.
●
The story is small enough to complete, integrate, and demo within the sprint.
2.2 Definition of Done
●
Built end to end in the shared codebase — no "we'll wire this up later" work left on the demonstrated
path.
●
Automated unit, integration, API contract, security, and relevant AI evaluation tests all pass.
●
Architecture boundaries, audit events, observability, and feature flags are in place.
●
The responsive desktop and mobile-browser flow has been tested.
●
Acceptance criteria are demonstrated live, using a real role and real persisted data — not a mock.
●
Demo feedback and defects are captured, and documentation/backlog are updated accordingly.
●
No unresolved critical security, privacy, data-integrity, or confidentiality defect remains.
2.3 Mandatory Sprint Demo Standard
Every sprint demo, including this one, has to follow the same script:
●
Start from the current deployed staging product — not a local prototype.
●
Use an end-user role and complete a task that actually means something; if the work is operations-only,
show an observable operational capability instead.
●
Show persisted state, and show how it affects the next stage of the workflow.
●
Show at least one happy path and one exception/failure path for whatever new capability is being
demoed.
●
Show the new flow on desktop and on at least one mobile-browser viewport.
●
Show the relevant audit/AI-decision evidence for anything autonomous.
●
Capture stakeholder observations as backlog items before the review closes.
●
If the integrated demo can't actually run, the increment isn't accepted as Done — no exceptions.

Each requirement below traces back to a Sprint 1 increment item in Section 15.5, filled out using the matching
detailed requirement, architecture module, and ADR from the Master PRD (Sections 6, 8, and 14).
FR-S1-01 — Account Registration, Login and Verification (from REQ-01)
Source: Master PRD Section 6, REQ-01 — Authentication, Roles and Consent.
What it covers: users need to be able to register and log in using either email or Google, verify their email,
optionally verify a phone number, reset a forgotten password, and log out cleanly.
AI-native behaviour: the AI greets users and routes them by role, explains consent in plain language rather than
legal boilerplate, notices when onboarding has been left half-finished and nudges the user toward the next step.
If something looks risky, the system can quietly ask for step-up verification without exposing its internal
reasoning.
User control and boundaries: users are in control of their own consent and can withdraw anything non-essential.
Admin actions should never surface to a regular user, under any circumstance.
Acceptance criteria: a new buyer or seller should be able to create an account, verify their identity channels,
give consent, log in and out, and land on the correct responsive dashboard — all within five minutes, all through
versioned APIs, and all passing mobile-browser tests.
FR-S1-02 — Role Selection and Buyer/Seller Dashboards (from REQ-01)
Source: Master PRD Section 6 (REQ-01) and the "buyer/seller dashboards" line item in Section 15.5.
What it covers: at onboarding, users choose whether they're a Buyer or a Seller and get routed to the dashboard
built for that role. Admin and exception-handling users are never created through self-service — only through
controlled internal provisioning.
User control and boundaries: admin actions and admin-only screens must stay completely invisible to normal
Buyer/Seller users.
Acceptance criteria: a registered user reaches their correct role dashboard, on both desktop and mobile-browser
viewports, on live staging.
FR-S1-03 — Consent Capture and Management (from REQ-01)
Source: Master PRD Section 6, REQ-01.
What it covers: at onboarding, we capture consent records for AI use, verification processing, communications,
and document sharing.
AI-native behaviour: before capturing consent, the AI explains each item in plain language so the user actually
understands what they're agreeing to.
User control and boundaries: users can withdraw consent for anything non-essential after their account is
created.
Acceptance criteria: consent is recorded per category, visible to the user, and withdrawable where non-essential
— and all of that needs to check out against the persisted state, not just the UI.

FR-S1-04 — Responsive Web Shell and Mobile-Browser Support (from REQ-16)
Source: Master PRD Section 6, REQ-16 — Mobile-Portability and Client-Neutral Product Design (introduced this
sprint); Section 14 (Mobile Portability and Architecture Evolution).
What it covers: the MVP ships as a responsive website, but every Sprint 1 capability — registration, login, role
dashboards — needs to be reachable through versioned, role-secured, task-specific APIs. Nothing should depend
on web-session-only logic.
AI-native behaviour: onboarding and AI guidance run as task APIs with resumable state, not as a chat session
tied to one browser tab.
User control and boundaries: the web presentation can vary however it needs to, but no business rule or source-
of-truth state is allowed to live only in the browser.
Acceptance criteria: all Sprint 1 flows pass both desktop and mobile-browser tests, and registration, login, and
dashboard retrieval are demonstrably callable through the same versioned APIs the web client itself uses.
FR-S1-05 — Versioned, API-First Foundation
Source: the "versioned API" line in Master PRD Section 15.5; ADR-016 (API-first, client-neutral business logic);
Section 10.3 (API and client-contract principles).
What it covers: every Sprint 1 capability sits behind a versioned API contract owned by the Auth & Identity
module, independent of whatever the web presentation layer looks like.
Why it matters: this is what lets us pull out a native client later (Section 14) without having to rework the
backend.
Acceptance criteria: every Sprint 1 endpoint carries an API version, and a client-neutral request can complete
authentication without ever touching the web UI.
FR-S1-06 — CI/CD Pipeline and Shared Staging Environment
Source: the "CI/CD; staging" line in Master PRD Section 15.5; Section 15.3 Definition of Done; Section 15.4 demo
standard.
What it covers: an automated CI/CD pipeline builds, tests, and deploys the product to a shared staging
environment, and that's the environment every sprint demo runs from — per Section 15.4's rule to "start from
the current deployed staging product, not a local prototype."
Acceptance criteria: a change merged to mainline gets automatically built, tested, and deployed to staging, and
the Sprint 1 demo runs from that deployed environment.
FR-S1-07 — Audit Skeleton (foundational)
Source: the "audit skeleton" line in Master PRD Section 15.5; Section 8.1's "Audit & Observability" bounded
context (owns append-only audit records, traces, metrics, and quality samples); Section 16.1's cross-cutting NFR
"Auditability and immutable events," introduced this sprint.

What it covers: we stand up a minimal, append-only audit capability so that Auth & Identity events —
registration, login, logout, consent capture/withdrawal, role selection — are recorded starting in Sprint 1, ahead

Acceptance criteria: for a demo user, the registration, login/logout, consent, and role-selection events all show
up in an append-only audit log tied to that user's account.

4.1 Architecture Constraints Applicable to Sprint 1

●
The Auth & Identity module owns accounts, roles, sessions, consent, and authentication state — it must
not own listings, pricing, offers, or verification-evidence decisions (Section 8.1).
●
The Audit & Observability module owns append-only audit records, traces, metrics, and quality samples
— it must not own operational business decisions (Section 8.1).
●
ADR-001/002: build on a microservice-ready modular monolith; don't start with full microservices.
●
ADR-008: use internal domain events inside the monolith to support audit, notifications, debugging, and
later extraction.
●
ADR-016: keep business logic API-first and client-neutral, so mobile portability doesn't require backend
rework down the line.
●
ADR-018: Sprint 1 needs to produce a live, usable, integrated increment — not slides, wireframes, or a
set of APIs sitting in isolation.
5. Sprint 1 Acceptance Criteria and Demo Script Outline
Pulled together from the Sprint 1 row of Section 15.5 and the acceptance criteria under REQ-01 and REQ-16
(Section 6).
●

●

dashboard.
●
Demonstrate password reset and logout for at least one role.
●
Demonstrate a consent capture and a consent withdrawal for a non-essential processing category.
●
Show the same registration, login, and dashboard-retrieval actions succeeding through the versioned
API, independent of the web UI — the client-neutral proof required by REQ-16.
●
Show the audit log entries created by the demoed registration, login, and consent events.
●
Show one exception/failure path — an invalid verification code, or a failed login attempt — alongside
the happy path, per the mandatory demo standard.
●
Confirm the whole demo runs from the shared staging environment deployed via CI/CD, not from a local
build.
